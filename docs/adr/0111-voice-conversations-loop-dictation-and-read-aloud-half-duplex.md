# Voice conversations loop Dictation and Read aloud, half-duplex, and only the owner starts or resumes them

Issue #90 asks to extend Dictation and Read aloud into an explicitly started hands-free conversation: listening, thinking and speaking indicators, turn detection with manual controls, a transcript of what reaches Hermes, interruption, device selection, mute and End, without any background microphone or automatic restart.

## What the server offers (checked in source, upstream `c419cf7`; the deployed `bf0203a` per the research doc, "Voice")

- `POST /api/audio/transcribe` and `POST /api/audio/speak`, the routes Dictation and Read aloud already use and test, take a finished recording and whole text. Neither detects turns.
- `/api/audio/transcribe-stream` and `/api/audio/speak-stream` stream partial transcripts and PCM speech over WebSockets. Each needs a socket ticket minted for it (`/api/webapp/socket-ticket`), which this app's Rust transport doesn't fetch or relay. The transcription stream has no end-of-turn event either: the client sends `{"eos": true}` when it decides the owner has finished.
- `/api/audio/voice-live/session` is GPT-Live, a separate OpenAI model that owns the microphone over WebRTC and hands requests to Hermes. It needs an OpenAI key and is not Hermes's chained STT → turn → TTS.
- Hermes's own voice mode ends a turn where the microphone is, by loudness: `AudioRecorder._track_speech` and `_should_auto_stop` (`tools/voice_mode.py`), with the profile's `voice.silence_threshold` (default 200, the RMS of 16-bit samples), `voice.silence_duration` (default 3.0 s) and `voice.max_recording_seconds` (default 120). The official desktop app does the same in its renderer.

## Decisions

- **A conversation is Dictation and Read aloud in a loop, on the tested routes.** The microphone records until the turn ends, Hermes transcribes the WAV (`POST /api/audio/transcribe`), the text goes to the chat as the owner's message through `Hermes.send` (as a slash command's message does, so the typed Draft stays as it is and a failure is a Failed message), and when Hermes's reply ends it is spoken with the pane's Speaker (`POST /api/audio/speak`). The streaming sockets and GPT-Live aren't used: they would need a new ticketed socket relay in Rust, and the loop works without them. Lower latency through them is a follow-up, not a gap.
- **Turns end the way Hermes's voice mode ends them.** `TurnDetector` (`voiceTurns.ts`) is a port of Hermes's detector: speech is confirmed after 0.3 s above the threshold, dips under 0.3 s are ridden out, and the turn ends after `voice.silence_duration` of quiet or at `voice.max_recording_seconds`. Rust reads the three numbers from the profile's config with the provider names, and nothing else of it (`Dashboard::voice_setup`). A value Hermes wouldn't use (not a number, `true`, not positive) falls back to Hermes's default. The quiet is kept between 0.5 s and 30 s. A turn never exceeds Dictation's five-minute limit, even when Hermes sets none (0 or less). Send now ends the turn at once; Discard drops it.
- **Half-duplex, as the echo guard.** While any audio plays in the pane (the conversation's reply or the owner's own Read aloud), and for 0.6 s after, the microphone's samples are thrown away and the turn so far is dropped. Speech can't interrupt Hermes by voice. The owner interrupts with Stop speaking, or Esc on the panel. As a second guard, a transcript of at least four words that is a run of the reply's own words, from speech that began within 3 s of the audio ending, is shown as "Not sent" and not sent. Shorter answers ("yes, go ahead") are never taken as an echo.
- **Stopping the audio and stopping the reply are separate controls.** Stop speaking stops the audio only: the reply stays in the chat, and the conversation listens again. Stop reply (while Hermes thinks) is the composer's Stop: Hermes interrupts the run, and a reply that ended interrupted, failed, or had no words isn't spoken.
- **It doesn't listen while the chat is busy otherwise.** While a reply it didn't ask for runs (a typed or queued message), or an Approval waits, it listens to nothing, and that reply isn't spoken. A transcript that arrives once the chat is busy is shown as not sent. Approvals are answered on their cards as always.
- **Only the owner starts or resumes it.** Start voice conversation sits beside Dictation's button and opens the microphone only when clicked. It needs Hermes connected, both audio routes, `stt.enabled`, and a chat that has started (a new chat's first message makes the chat, and the pane for it is a different one). Dictation and the conversation are never on at once.
- **The pause policy.** The conversation pauses, closing the microphone and stopping the audio, when Hermes disconnects, the window is hidden to the tray or minimized (the page's visibility), or its pane stops being the focused one (the other pane, Settings, or another screen in front). Pausing keeps what reached Hermes on screen. A turn Hermes was transcribing is held, to send with Try again. A message already on its way still goes and its fate shows, but its reply isn't spoken. Nothing resumes it by itself. A reconnect, the window coming back, or focus returning only re-enables Resume listening, which reopens the microphone when clicked.
- **It ends with its pane.** End, opening another chat, switching profile or server, signing out or closing the app all end it: the microphone and audio system close, the audio element is released and its blob URL revoked, and answers still on their way are dropped by a session counter. End puts focus back on the Start button.
- **Each recording is sent at most once.** A turn is numbered when it ends. Only that number's first transcript goes to the chat, and a turn can end only once, from listening.
- **Devices and permission.** The panel lists the system's audio inputs (`enumerateDevices`) and reopens the one picked with `deviceId: {exact}`. The pick lasts for this run of the app and isn't stored. Speakers follow the system default: WebKitGTK has no `setSinkId`. A refused or missing microphone, or one unplugged mid-conversation, pauses it with the reason. Nothing reopens until the owner picks another microphone or resumes.
- **Bounded memory, and no audio on disk.** Waiting for speech keeps half a second of audio. A turn is at most its limit. One failed turn is held for Try again. "What Hermes got" keeps the last 20 entries, in memory, and End clears it. Speech is one blob at a time. No audio is ever written on this computer. The text a turn becomes is a chat message sent through `Hermes.send`, so this computer keeps it as it keeps a typed message: in the outbox while it goes, as a Failed message if Hermes doesn't take it, and in the chat's Saved copy. Ending the conversation doesn't remove it, as sending a typed message can't be undone here either.

## Considered options

- **The streaming STT/TTS sockets.** Rejected for now. They need a socket-ticket relay through Rust (the webview can't hold the dashboard's credentials), and their only gain is latency. The loop on the tested routes meets the issue. Recorded as a follow-up.
- **Voice barge-in (speech interrupting the reply).** Rejected. It would need the microphone to listen while the speakers play, which on a laptop without verified echo cancellation feeds Hermes's own voice back as turns. The official desktop app runs a separate tuned monitor for this. Interruption by button and key is reliable.
- **A local voice-activity model or local speech-to-text.** Rejected: the issue rules out desktop-local substitutes for server resources, and Hermes's own loudness detector is the supported one.
- **Closing the microphone between turns.** Rejected: reopening it each turn adds the audio system's start-up to every turn, and on some setups a permission prompt. It stays open while the conversation is on, shows "Mic on", and its samples are discarded except while listening.
- **Keeping the conversation running in an unfocused or hidden pane.** Rejected. The owner couldn't see that the microphone was open, which the issue forbids.

## Gaps

- Real microphones, speakers and their echo can't be exercised in the automated tests. They use a stand-in microphone and audio element, and the half-duplex guard is what keeps echo out. Physical-device testing is listed in the pull request.
- Replies are spoken only once complete, and whole: the REST speech route has no streaming.
- The threshold is in Hermes's units, but browsers apply automatic gain control and noise suppression before the app sees the samples, so the same `voice.silence_threshold` can behave differently here than with the CLI's microphone.
